Specialized Cells

Connect cell structure to function without assuming every cell contains equal amounts of every organelle.

45–60 minutesTier 1 · Home-safePortfolio Evidence

Learning objectives

  • Explain specialization
  • Relate structures to cell function
  • Recognize trade-offs

Materials

  • Specialized cell table
  • Notebook
Tier 1 · Home-safe

Use only the stated materials and conditions. Stop whenever equipment, supervision, accessibility, or the environment differs from the written plan.

1. Division of labor

Cells in a multicellular organism share genetic information but express different gene patterns, producing different structures and functions. Specialization creates efficiency and interdependence.

2. Examples

CellAdaptationFunction
Root hairLong projectionAbsorption surface
PalisadeMany chloroplastsPhotosynthesis
Red bloodBiconcave; hemoglobinOxygen transport
NeuronLong processesCommunication
MuscleContractile proteinsForce production

3. Trade-offs

Mature human red blood cells lack a nucleus and most organelles, increasing space for hemoglobin but preventing division and repair.

Portfolio evidence

Choose two specialized cells. Explain one adaptation, one function, and one trade-off for each.
