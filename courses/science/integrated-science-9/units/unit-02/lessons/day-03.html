Unit 02 · Day 3

Prokaryotic Cells

Examine bacterial organization as a successful cellular system with membrane, cytoplasm, DNA, ribosomes, and diverse adaptations.

45–60 minutesTier 1 · Home-safePortfolio Evidence

Learning objectives

  • Identify structures common to typical prokaryotic cells
  • Compare nucleoid DNA with a membrane-bound nucleus
  • Explain why prokaryotic does not mean primitive or unfinished

Materials

  • Notebook
  • Bacterial cell model page
  • Colored pencils optional
Tier 1 · Home-safe

Use only the stated materials and conditions. Stop whenever equipment, supervision, accessibility, or the environment differs from the written plan.

1. Core organization

A typical bacterial cell contains a plasma membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes, and DNA in a nucleoid region. Many also have a cell wall. Capsules, pili, flagella, plasmids, and internal membrane systems are not universal.

2. Comparison without hierarchy

Prokaryotes and eukaryotes have different cellular organizations. Existing bacteria are not ancestors frozen in time; they are modern organisms shaped by evolution.

Correct the misconception

Rewrite “bacteria are simple failed animal cells” as an accurate statement.
