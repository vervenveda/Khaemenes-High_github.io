Unit 02 · Day 10

Cell Size & Surface Area

Use surface-area-to-volume models to explain exchange limits, cell size, membrane folds, and multicellular transport systems.

45–60 minutesTier 1 · Home-safePortfolio Evidence

Learning objectives

  • Calculate cube surface area, volume, and ratio
  • Explain why volume increases faster than surface area
  • Connect cell shape to exchange function

Materials

  • Surface-area calculator
  • Calculator
  • Paper cube nets or blocks optional
Tier 1 · Home-safe

Use only the stated materials and conditions. Stop whenever equipment, supervision, accessibility, or the environment differs from the written plan.

1. Cube model

For a cube of side length s, surface area = 6s² and volume = s³. The ratio simplifies to 6:s.

Side 1: 6:1. Side 2: 24:8 = 3:1.

2. Biological meaning

As cell size increases, exchange demand grows with volume while membrane area grows more slowly. Cells may remain small, flatten, elongate, form folds, develop projections, or rely on transport systems.

Limitation: Real cells are not perfect cubes, and metabolic demand differs.
