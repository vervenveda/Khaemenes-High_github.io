Unit 02 · Day 1

Cell Theory & Evidence

Use observations, experiments, and repeated microscopy evidence to explain why cells are considered the basic unit of life.

45–60 minutesTier 1 · Home-safePortfolio Evidence

Learning objectives

  • State the major claims of modern cell theory
  • Distinguish direct observation from inference
  • Explain why viruses are described as acellular rather than complete cells

Materials

  • Science notebook
  • Cell-theory evidence timeline
  • Optional hand lens
Tier 1 · Home-safe

Use only the stated materials and conditions. Stop whenever equipment, supervision, accessibility, or the environment differs from the written plan.

1. The claims

  • Living organisms are composed of one or more cells.
  • The cell is the smallest unit that carries out the full set of processes associated with life.
  • New cells arise from pre-existing cells through cell division.
  • Cells contain hereditary information passed during division.
Scientific theory: a broad explanatory framework supported by multiple lines of evidence, not a guess.

2. Evidence and history

Early microscopes revealed repeating compartments. Later observations showed living cells, microorganisms, nuclei, and cell division. Experiments against spontaneous generation strengthened the conclusion that cells arise from existing cells.

Evidence sort

Classify each statement as observation or inference: “compartments are visible,” “the compartments are cells,” “a new cell appeared after division,” and “all cells come from earlier cells.” Explain one choice.

3. Viruses and the boundary of life

Viruses contain genetic material and evolve, but they are not complete cells and depend on host-cell machinery for replication.

Portfolio evidence

Write a four-sentence explanation of cell theory that includes one observation, one inference, and one limitation or boundary.
