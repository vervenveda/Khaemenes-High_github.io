Unit 01 · Required Investigation

Paper Helicopter: Design, Drop & Measure

Test how rotor length affects mean fall time while maintaining a safe, transparent, repeatable method.

Tier 15+ Trials per ConditionMean · Range · Graph · CER
Safety and stop conditions

Use scissors normally and safely. Use a clear, level drop area away from stairs, fans, open windows, pets, people, and fragile objects. Stop when airflow or interruptions prevent a fair comparison.

Question and hypothesis

Core question: How does rotor length affect the mean fall time of a paper helicopter dropped from a fixed height?

Variables and controls

Independent variableRotor length (cm)
Dependent variableFall time (s), operationally defined from release to first contact with the landing surface
ControlsPaper, body size, rotor width, mass or paper clips, drop height, release method, location, timing method
Minimum designAt least three rotor lengths and five trials per length

Method

  1. Create paper helicopters identical except for rotor length.
  2. Measure and record each rotor length.
  3. Mark a fixed drop height.
  4. Release without pushing or spinning.
  5. Measure time from release to first landing contact.
  6. Record each raw trial immediately.
  7. Repeat at least five times per rotor length.
  8. Calculate mean and range for each condition.
  9. Graph rotor length against mean fall time.
  10. Write a CER conclusion and evaluate limitations.

Analysis and evaluation

  1. Describe the pattern using actual values.
  2. Identify variation within each condition.
  3. Document any anomaly and what was checked.
  4. State whether the evidence supports the hypothesis.
  5. Identify the most important limitation.
  6. Propose one specific improvement and explain how it strengthens the evidence.
