Variables & Operational Definitions

Name the independent, dependent, and controlled variables, and define exactly how each measured variable will be observed.

45–60 minutesTier 1 · Home-safePortfolio Evidence

Learning objectives

  • Identify independent, dependent, and controlled variables
  • Write an operational definition
  • Recognize when variables are confounded

Materials

  • Science notebook
  • Provided scenarios
  • Calculator optional
Tier 1 · Home-safe

Use only the stated materials and conditions. Stop when the environment, equipment, supervision, or learner needs differ from the written activity.

1. Variable roles

  • Independent variable: deliberately changed or used to form comparison groups.
  • Dependent variable: measured outcome.
  • Controlled variable: factor kept sufficiently consistent.
  • Confounding variable: alternative difference that may explain the result.
Question: How does paper-helicopter rotor length affect fall time?
Independent: rotor length
Dependent: fall time in seconds
Controls: paper, body dimensions, paper clips, drop height, release method, location

2. Operational definitions

An operational definition states how a variable will be measured or classified. “Plant health” is vague. “Change in stem height in centimeters from Day 1 to Day 14” is operational.

Practice

Write operational definitions for “water clarity,” “reaction time,” and “paper absorbency.”

3. Confounding example

A student compares plants in sunlight with one soil type to plants in shade with another soil type. Light and soil differ together, so the effects are confounded.

Portfolio evidence

Choose one investigation question from Day 1. List its independent, dependent, controlled, and possible confounding variables.
