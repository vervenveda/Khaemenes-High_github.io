Unit 01 · Day 3

Fair Tests, Controls & Sampling

Use controls, comparison conditions, repeated trials, representative sampling, and randomization when appropriate.

45–60 minutesTier 1 · Home-safePortfolio Evidence

Learning objectives

  • Explain the purpose of a control or comparison condition
  • Distinguish replication from sample size
  • Identify selection bias and improve a sample

Materials

  • Science notebook
  • Coins or numbered slips optional
  • Provided sampling scenario
Tier 1 · Home-safe

Use only the stated materials and conditions. Stop when the environment, equipment, supervision, or learner needs differ from the written activity.

1. Fair comparison

A fair test changes the intended independent variable while reducing alternative explanations. “Fair” does not mean perfect; it means the design is defensible and transparent.

  • A control condition provides a baseline.
  • Repeated trials reveal variation within a condition.
  • Replication asks whether a result appears again in new trials or samples.
  • Random assignment can reduce systematic group differences.

2. Sampling

A sample should represent the population relevant to the question. Surveying only volunteers from one club may not represent an entire school.

Sampling redesign

A student wants to estimate average backpack mass for Grade 9 but measures only five students arriving early for sports practice. Identify two limitations and propose a stronger sampling method.

3. More trials do not repair every flaw

Repeating a biased or confounded method many times may produce a precise answer to the wrong question. Design quality and quantity of data both matter.

Portfolio evidence

Revise yesterday’s investigation by adding a comparison condition, trial count, and sampling plan where relevant.
