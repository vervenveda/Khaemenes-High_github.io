Unit 01 · Day 1

From Phenomena to Questions

Transform broad curiosity into a focused scientific question that can be answered with observations or measurements.

45–60 minutesTier 1 · Home-safePortfolio Evidence

Learning objectives

  • Distinguish broad topics from investigable questions
  • Identify measurable variables in a question
  • Revise subjective wording into operational wording

Materials

  • Science notebook
  • Pencil or keyboard
  • Three ordinary phenomena from home, school, or the environment
Tier 1 · Home-safe

Use only the stated materials and conditions. Stop when the environment, equipment, supervision, or learner needs differ from the written activity.

1. Begin with a phenomenon

A phenomenon is an observable event, pattern, or process that invites explanation. Curiosity may begin broadly, but an investigation requires a question whose evidence can be collected.

Broad topic: paper airplanes
Investigable question: How does wing length affect flight distance when paper type, fold pattern, release height, and launch method are kept consistent?

2. Testable-question checklist

  • Names a factor that varies or categories being compared.
  • Names an outcome that can be observed or measured.
  • Can be investigated safely with available materials and time.
  • Does not depend on personal taste unless preference itself is the measured subject.
  • Is narrow enough to produce interpretable evidence.
Revision practice

Rewrite “Which type of music is best for studying?” as a question with a defined participant group, listening condition, study task, and measured outcome.

3. Scientific questions are not all experiments

Some questions are answered by controlled experiments. Others require observation, fieldwork, historical records, models, surveys, or existing datasets. The method must fit the question.

Portfolio evidence

Write one experimental question and one observational question about a topic you care about. Identify the evidence each would require.
