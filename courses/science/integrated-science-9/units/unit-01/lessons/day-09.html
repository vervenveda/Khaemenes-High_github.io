Unit 01 · Day 9

Claim, Evidence, Reasoning & Sources

Construct an explanation that links relevant evidence to a scientific principle and evaluate whether a source makes its method and limitations visible.

45–60 minutesTier 1 · Home-safePortfolio Evidence

Learning objectives

  • Distinguish claim, evidence, and reasoning
  • Select relevant evidence
  • Evaluate source transparency, expertise, conflicts, and corroboration

Materials

  • Science notebook
  • One science-related media claim
  • CER template
Tier 1 · Home-safe

Use only the stated materials and conditions. Stop when the environment, equipment, supervision, or learner needs differ from the written activity.

1. CER structure

  • Claim: direct answer to the question.
  • Evidence: relevant observations or data.
  • Reasoning: scientific principle explaining why the evidence supports the claim.
Claim: Longer rotors increased mean fall time in this investigation.
Evidence: Mean fall time increased from 1.2 s at 4 cm to 1.8 s at 8 cm.
Reasoning: Greater rotor area can increase air resistance, reducing downward speed under the tested conditions.

2. Source-evaluation questions

  • Who produced the claim and what expertise is relevant?
  • Are method, sample, data, and uncertainty available?
  • Is the source reporting original evidence or repeating another source?
  • Are conflicts of interest disclosed?
  • Do independent credible sources broadly agree?
  • Does the headline overstate the evidence?

3. Portfolio evidence

Source audit

Record a scientific claim, its original source if available, the evidence provided, one strength, one limitation, and whether the wording matches the evidence.
