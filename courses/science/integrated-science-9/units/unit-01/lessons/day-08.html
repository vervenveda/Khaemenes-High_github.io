Patterns, Uncertainty & Causation

Interpret trends and anomalies, distinguish random from systematic error, and separate association from causal evidence.

45–60 minutesTier 1 · Home-safePortfolio Evidence

Learning objectives

  • Distinguish random and systematic error
  • Explain why correlation alone does not prove causation

Materials

Tier 1 · Home-safe

1. Random and systematic effects

Random error contributes unpredictable variation among repeated measurements. Systematic error shifts results consistently because of calibration, method, or design bias.

Human reaction time may add random variation to stopwatch measurements. A stopwatch that always reads 0.2 s high creates a systematic offset.

2. Association is not automatically cause

Two variables may be associated because one affects the other, the direction is reversed, a third variable affects both, selection bias is present, or coincidence occurs.

Reasoning

Students carrying umbrellas are more common on days with wet pavement. Explain why umbrellas do not necessarily cause wet pavement and identify a third variable.

3. Dataset laboratory

Analyze the fictional shade and surface-temperature observations. The data can support an association in the observed sites, but the observational design alone cannot isolate every possible cause.
