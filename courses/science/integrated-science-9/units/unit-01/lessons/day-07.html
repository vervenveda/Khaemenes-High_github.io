Unit 01 · Day 7

Tables, Graphs & Scale

Construct complete tables, select an appropriate graph, label axes and units, and recognize visual choices that distort interpretation.

45–60 minutesTier 1 · Home-safePortfolio Evidence

Learning objectives

  • Create a complete data table
  • Choose among bar, line, and scatter graphs
  • Evaluate axis scale and labeling

Materials

  • Science notebook
  • Graph paper or spreadsheet
  • Ruler
  • Paper-helicopter results
Tier 1 · Home-safe

Use only the stated materials and conditions. Stop when the environment, equipment, supervision, or learner needs differ from the written activity.

1. Complete tables

Table headings name the variable and unit. Raw data remain separate from calculated values. Avoid placing units repeatedly in every cell when the unit belongs in the heading.

Complete heading: Fall time (s)
Incomplete heading: Results

2. Choose the display

  • Bar graph: numerical values across discrete categories.
  • Line graph: change across ordered time or another continuous sequence when connecting values is meaningful.
  • Scatter plot: relationship between two numerical variables; a trend line may summarize association.
Graph plan

Identify the best graph for your helicopter results. State the x-axis variable and unit, y-axis variable and unit, title, and scale.

3. Visual integrity

A truncated axis is not automatically wrong, but it can make small differences look dramatic. A reader must inspect the scale, intervals, omitted values, labels, and whether uncertainty is shown.

Portfolio evidence

Create the final graph and write one sentence describing the pattern without claiming more than the data show.
