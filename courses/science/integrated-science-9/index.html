Course design

One connected scientific foundation.

This is an integrated science course—not a biology-only sequence. Students investigate living systems, matter, forces, energy, Earth, climate, space, engineering, and the quality of scientific evidence.

Foundation

Visual models, language support, smaller steps, guided calculations, scaffolded investigations, and additional examples.

Core

The complete Grade 9 course, laboratory portfolio, assessments, source evaluation, and sustainable engineering capstone.

Extended

Honors depth, unfamiliar applications, independent investigations, advanced datasets, research, and deeper scientific argument.

Course outcomes

  • Explain important concepts across life, chemical, physical, Earth, environmental, and space science.
  • Plan safe and ethical investigations and evaluate the quality of methods and evidence.
  • Use SI units, measurements, graphs, models, calculations, public datasets, and scientific sources.
  • Distinguish observation, inference, correlation, causation, uncertainty, and unsupported claims.
  • Design and improve solutions while considering safety, cost, accessibility, culture, and environmental effects.

Scientific domains

Connected knowledge across disciplines

Students repeatedly connect structures, systems, matter, energy, evidence, scale, cause and effect, stability, and change.

L

Life Science

Cells, body systems, inheritance, ecosystems, evolution, biodiversity, and human impacts.

M

Chemistry

Atomic structure, periodic patterns, bonding, reactions, conservation, acids, bases, and materials.

E

Physics

Motion, forces, energy, circuits, waves, light, sound, communication, measurement, and modeling.

⊕

Earth & Space

Earth systems, cycles, geology, hazards, climate evidence, planetary motion, gravity, and light.

Q

Scientific Inquiry

Questions, variables, fair tests, fieldwork, data quality, uncertainty, explanations, and peer review.

D

Engineering Design

Criteria, constraints, prototypes, testing, optimization, trade-offs, sustainability, and communication.

i

Evidence Literacy

Sources, graphs, statistics, misinformation, conflicts of interest, correlation, causation, and uncertainty.

⚖

Science & Society

Ethics, culture, public health, technology, environmental responsibility, policy, and informed decisions.

Weekly learning rhythm

Observe, understand, investigate, explain

The five-lesson rhythm can be rearranged when laboratory work needs a longer continuous block.

1

Phenomenon

Observe, ask questions, predict, and identify what is not yet understood.

2

Concept & Model

Read, discuss, diagram, calculate, and connect vocabulary to a scientific model.

3

Practice & Evidence

Use examples, data, sources, graphs, equations, and representations.

4

Investigation

Conduct a safe hands-on, field, simulation, engineering, or dataset activity.

5

Synthesis

Explain, assess, correct, reflect, and add evidence to the portfolio.

Balanced assessment

Four equal dimensions of scientific mastery

Knowledge alone is not sufficient; students must also investigate, interpret evidence, and understand scientific impacts.

25%

Knowledge & Application

Scientific concepts, models, calculations, explanations, and transfer.

25%

Inquiry & Design

Questions, hypotheses, variables, procedures, safety, and ethical methods.

25%

Data & Evaluation

Tables, graphs, patterns, uncertainty, anomalies, limitations, and improvements.

25%

Impact & Communication

Evidence-based communication, ethics, culture, environment, and society.

Laboratory access

Rigorous science at different facility levels

Every investigation declares its safety tier, supervision, hazards, cleanup, accessibility alternatives, and stop conditions.

Tier 1 · Home-Safe

Low-risk observations, models, measurement, household-material activities, field observations, and public datasets.

Tier 2 · Adult Supervision

Heat, glassware, tools, low-voltage circuits, biological samples, or household chemicals requiring direct oversight.

Tier 3 · Qualified Laboratory

Specialized equipment or hazards taught through a qualified facility, demonstration, dataset, video, or safe simulation.
