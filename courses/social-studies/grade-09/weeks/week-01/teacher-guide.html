Week 1: How We Know the Past

Essential Question: How can we build a trustworthy account of the past when evidence is incomplete or disputed?

Standards

  • D1.5.9-12 — Determine the kinds of sources helpful in answering compelling and supporting questions.
  • D2.His.1.9-12 — Evaluate how historical events and developments were shaped by unique circumstances and broader contexts.
  • D3.1.9-12 — Gather relevant information from multiple sources using origin, authority, structure, context, and corroborative value.
  • D2.Civ.10.9-12 — Analyze the impact and appropriate roles of personal interests and perspectives in civic life.
  • D4.1.9-12 — Construct arguments using precise claims and evidence while acknowledging counterclaims.
  • CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.RH.9-10.1 — Cite specific textual evidence to support analysis of primary and secondary sources.
  • CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.RH.9-10.7 — Integrate quantitative or technical analysis with qualitative analysis.
  • CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.WHST.9-10.1 — Write arguments focused on discipline-specific content.

Assignment Guidance

1. Source Lab — Conflicting Eyewitness Accounts

Teacher note: Accept multiple interpretations when claims are supported. Do not award full evidence credit for summary without analysis.

Sample-answer guidance: A strong response will identify the source’s origin and audience, use at least two specific details, and explain how the source contributes to the question “How can we build a trustworthy account of the past when evidence is incomplete or disputed?” while naming one limitation.

2. Atlas and Data Investigation

Teacher note: Check labels, legend, scale or units, and whether the student distinguishes observation from explanation.

Sample-answer guidance: A strong product uses accurate labels and units, identifies a meaningful pattern, proposes an evidence-based explanation, and notes what the visualization cannot prove.

3. Evidence-Based Seminar or Argument

Teacher note: Score the quality of reasoning rather than the political or moral position selected.

Sample-answer guidance: Answers may reasonably take different positions. Full-credit work directly answers “Is an openly biased source less useful than a source that claims to be neutral?,” uses accurate evidence from at least two source types, and addresses a serious counterargument.

Constructed Response

Using at least two specific pieces of evidence, answer: How can we build a trustworthy account of the past when evidence is incomplete or disputed?

Exemplar: A full-credit response states a defensible answer to the essential question, uses two accurate details from the week, explains how each detail supports the claim, and acknowledges one complexity or limitation.
