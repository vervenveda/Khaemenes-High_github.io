Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

Maya cities combined dynastic rule, trade, agriculture, writing, calendrical knowledge, and regional rivalry. The Mexica built a tribute empire centered on Tenochtitlan, a large island city supported by causeways, markets, and chinampa agriculture. The Inca governed a vast Andean territory through roads, storehouses, labor obligations, local officials, and quipu records.

People, Power, and Systems

North American Mississippian societies built urban ceremonial centers such as Cahokia and linked communities through rivers and exchange. Pacific navigators settled islands across enormous distances using wayfinding traditions rooted in observation and teaching.

Reading the Evidence

Evidence includes codices, architecture, earthworks, oral traditions, quipu, ecological records, and colonial accounts. Colonial writers often misunderstood or deliberately recast Indigenous institutions, making source comparison essential.

Why It Matters

These histories demonstrate that complex societies do not require identical technologies or political forms. They also show the continuing presence and knowledge of Indigenous peoples rather than ending their history at European arrival.

Source Study: Recordkeeping Without Alphabetic Writing

Analyze a quipu description, codex image, mound plan, and navigation account. Evaluate what each system records and how knowledge is transmitted.

Vocabulary

tribute stateA state that requires subject communities to deliver goods, labor, or military support.
chinampaA highly productive raised-field farming system used in the Basin of Mexico.
quipuAn Andean knotted-cord recording system.
aylluAn Andean kin-based community with shared obligations and resources.
wayfindingNavigation through stars, swells, winds, clouds, wildlife, and accumulated knowledge.
oral historyA recorded or transmitted account based on spoken memory and testimony.

Key Understandings

  • Maya, Mexica, Inca, Mississippian, and Pacific societies developed sophisticated but different forms of urbanism and authority.
  • Indigenous knowledge systems included mathematics, astronomy, engineering, agriculture, recordkeeping, and navigation.
  • European colonial sources must be compared with archaeology, Indigenous accounts, and surviving cultural knowledge.
