Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

Adam Smith described how specialization and exchange could increase productivity, while also warning about monopoly and the effects of repetitive labor. Later advocates of laissez-faire emphasized private property and limited regulation. Industrial crises and inequality encouraged socialist, cooperative, and labor alternatives.

People, Power, and Systems

Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels argued that capitalist ownership produced class conflict and exploitation. Other socialists preferred democratic reform, cooperatives, or municipal ownership. Labor unions fought for shorter hours, safer workplaces, wages, and political rights.

Reading the Evidence

Economic debates should distinguish description, prediction, and moral judgment. A source may explain how markets operate, propose an ideal system, or defend the interests of a social group.

Why It Matters

Contemporary economies are mixed. Students learn to evaluate specific policies—minimum wages, workplace rules, public education, taxation, social insurance—rather than relying only on ideological labels.

Source Study: Economic Ideas and Worker Demands

Compare Smith, Marx and Engels, a cooperative reformer, and a labor petition. Identify each source’s problem, mechanism, and proposed solution.

Vocabulary

capitalismAn economic system based largely on private ownership, markets, wage labor, and investment.
laissez-faireThe view that government should interfere minimally in economic exchange.
socialismA family of ideas favoring social or public control of major productive resources and greater economic equality.
MarxismA theory emphasizing class conflict, historical change, and collective ownership of production.
collective bargainingNegotiation between workers acting together and employers.
social insurancePublic programs that spread risks such as illness, unemployment, disability, or old age.

Key Understandings

  • Capitalism and socialism contain diverse schools rather than one fixed model each.
  • Labor reform emerged through unions, cooperatives, political parties, religious movements, mutual aid, and government regulation.
  • Modern economies combine markets, public institutions, households, and nonprofit organizations in different proportions.
