Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

Rome grew from a city-state into a republic and then an empire surrounding the Mediterranean. Han China consolidated territories after the Qin and developed an imperial bureaucracy shaped by Confucian learning. Both ruled large populations, collected taxes, built infrastructure, and defended long borders.

People, Power, and Systems

Roman institutions included assemblies, magistrates, a senate, provincial governors, and eventually powerful emperors. Citizenship expanded over time. Han government relied on appointed officials, commanderies, household registration, and state involvement in strategic goods. Local families remained important in both systems.

Reading the Evidence

Evidence includes laws, coins, roads, tombs, census records, letters, official histories, and archaeological remains. Comparing evidence shows how imperial policies looked different from the capital than from a frontier village.

Why It Matters

Neither empire simply disappeared overnight. Political fragmentation, fiscal strain, warfare, disease, succession crises, migration, and local adaptation changed imperial structures. Later societies continued to use Roman law, Chinese bureaucratic traditions, languages, religions, and infrastructure.

Source Study: Law, Office, and Frontier Life

Compare Roman legal evidence with a Han administrative text and a frontier artifact. Ask how central rules reached local communities.

Vocabulary

republicA political system in which public authority is exercised through institutions rather than hereditary monarchy alone.
imperial bureaucracyA network of officials administering an empire.
civil serviceGovernment administration carried out by appointed or selected officials.
RomanizationThe uneven spread and local adaptation of Roman language, law, customs, and institutions.
Silk RoadsOverland and connected trade routes linking East Asia, Central Asia, Southwest Asia, and the Mediterranean.
continuity and changeA historical reasoning process that identifies what persisted and what transformed over time.

Key Understandings

  • Rome and Han China used roads, taxation, military force, law, and local elites to govern large territories.
  • Both empires promoted political order while incorporating diverse populations through different forms of citizenship and administration.
  • Their decline involved multiple interacting causes rather than a single collapse event.
