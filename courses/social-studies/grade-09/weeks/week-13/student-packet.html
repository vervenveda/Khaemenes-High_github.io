East Asia, 600–1450

Essential Question: How did states use education, technology, trade, and cultural borrowing to strengthen authority?

Student: _________________________________ Date: _______________

Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

Tang and Song China supported large cities, long-distance trade, tax administration, and educated officials. The examination system offered a route into office but required years of study and resources, so wealthy families remained advantaged. Printing widened access to texts while also strengthening standard curricula.

People, Power, and Systems

Commercial growth linked farms, workshops, canals, ports, and markets. Paper money, improved shipping, iron production, and urban consumption supported complex exchange. Technology developed through accumulated experimentation and state, military, and commercial demand.

Reading the Evidence

Korean kingdoms adopted writing, Buddhism, Confucian administration, and technologies while preserving local institutions. In Japan, court culture, landholding, warrior networks, and later shogunates produced a distinct political system.

Why It Matters

Cultural borrowing is active selection. Imported ideas change when local societies reinterpret them, and the borrowing society contributes innovations of its own.

Source Study: Examination, Court, and Warrior Sources

Compare an examination prompt, a Korean or Japanese court record, and evidence from a warrior household. Identify who gained authority through learning, lineage, or force.

Vocabulary

civil-service examinationA competitive system used in imperial China to select some officials through tested learning.
meritocracyA system that claims to assign status or office based on demonstrated ability.
Neo-ConfucianismA later development of Confucian thought shaped by philosophical and religious debate.
shogunateA Japanese military government led by a shogun.
tributary relationsDiplomatic and commercial relations expressed through ritual acknowledgment of hierarchy.
commercializationThe expansion of production and exchange for markets.

Key Understandings

  • Tang and Song governments expanded bureaucratic administration and examination culture, though elite families retained advantages.
  • Printing, gunpowder, navigation, iron production, and commercial growth changed East Asian economies and societies.
  • Korea and Japan adopted and adapted Chinese institutions rather than simply copying them.
