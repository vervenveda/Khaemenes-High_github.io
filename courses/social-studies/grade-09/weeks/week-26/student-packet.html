Industrial Revolution

Essential Question: Did industrialization improve human life, and for whom?

Student: _________________________________ Date: _______________

Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

Industrialization began earliest in Britain through an interaction of coal, commercial agriculture, capital, colonial connections, skilled labor, transport, and patent and market institutions. Textile machinery, steam power, iron production, and railways altered production and movement.

People, Power, and Systems

Factories concentrated workers and machines under schedules enforced by owners and supervisors. Women and children performed essential labor, often at lower wages. Artisans faced both displacement and new opportunities. Rapid urban growth overwhelmed housing, sanitation, and public health systems.

Reading the Evidence

Evidence includes factory testimony, wage books, census records, photographs, parliamentary reports, advertisements, and pollution data. Reform investigations sometimes exposed abuse while also reflecting middle-class assumptions about workers.

Why It Matters

Industrialization should not be reduced to progress or misery. It created wealth and longer-term improvements in many places while distributing gains and harms unequally. Economic growth and human welfare must be measured with more than total production.

Source Study: Factory Testimony and Production Data

Compare worker testimony, an owner’s defense, a reform report, and output data. Identify where perspectives and statistics align or conflict.

Vocabulary

industrializationThe expansion of machine production, factories, and fossil-fuel energy.
urbanizationGrowth in the proportion of people living in towns and cities.
division of laborThe separation of production into specialized tasks.
capitalWealth or productive resources invested to generate further value.
labor unionAn organization of workers formed to negotiate or act collectively.
externalityA cost or benefit imposed on people not directly included in an economic transaction.

Key Understandings

  • Industrialization depended on agriculture, capital, coal, technology, colonial trade, transport, labor, and institutions.
  • Factory production increased output while reorganizing time, skill, family labor, and workplace discipline.
  • Industrial growth raised long-run productivity but generated severe inequality, pollution, dangerous work, and urban hardship.
