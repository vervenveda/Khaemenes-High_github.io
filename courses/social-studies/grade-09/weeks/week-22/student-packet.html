Oceanic Exploration and the Columbian Exchange

Essential Question: How did oceanic expansion create new connections while producing conquest, demographic collapse, and ecological change?

Student: _________________________________ Date: _______________

Weekly Reading

Setting the Stage

Portuguese and Spanish voyages emerged from Mediterranean and Atlantic experience, African and Asian knowledge, ship design, navigation, and state-commercial competition. These voyages joined existing ocean worlds rather than creating global exchange from nothing.

People, Power, and Systems

In the Americas, Europeans encountered large, politically complex societies. Conquest depended on Indigenous allies, internal rivalries, weapons, disease, and contingent decisions. Indigenous peoples were not passive, and resistance continued for centuries.

Reading the Evidence

The Columbian Exchange moved maize, potatoes, cassava, tomatoes, horses, cattle, sugar, pathogens, and people. New crops supported population growth in some regions, while disease and colonial labor systems devastated communities elsewhere.

Why It Matters

Global connection cannot be evaluated only by the volume of trade. Historians must ask who controlled exchange, who carried the costs, and how ecological consequences reshaped daily life.

Source Study: Encounter Accounts in Conflict

Compare a European expedition account, a critic such as Las Casas, and an Indigenous or Indigenous-informed account. Identify agreement, motive, and silence.

Vocabulary

maritime technologyTools and knowledge used for navigation, shipbuilding, and ocean travel.
Columbian ExchangeThe movement of organisms, people, diseases, and goods between hemispheres after 1492.
conquistadorA Spanish conqueror involved in campaigns in the Americas.
encomiendaA Spanish colonial grant of Indigenous labor and tribute under claimed obligations of protection and conversion.
demographic collapseA severe decline in population.
ecological imperialismExpansion aided by transferred plants, animals, pathogens, and environmental change.

Key Understandings

  • European voyages built on Afro-Eurasian navigational knowledge, commercial competition, state finance, and maritime technology.
  • Indigenous American societies actively negotiated, resisted, allied, and adapted during encounters.
  • Disease, warfare, forced labor, and ecological change produced catastrophic population losses in many regions.
