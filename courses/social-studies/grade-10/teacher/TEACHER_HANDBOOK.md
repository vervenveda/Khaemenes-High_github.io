# Grade 10 United States History Honors — Teacher Handbook

## Course purpose
This one-credit, 36-week course studies United States history from Indigenous America and the founding era through the present, with the largest share of instructional time devoted to Reconstruction through contemporary America.

## Weekly rhythm
1. Monday — chronology, geography, and inquiry.
2. Tuesday — source analysis and corroboration.
3. Wednesday — maps, data, and quantitative reasoning.
4. Thursday — seminar and evidence-based writing.
5. Friday — synthesis, quiz, and corrections.

## Scoring
- Principal assignments: 45%
- Weekly quizzes: 20%
- Midterm: 12%
- Final: 18%
- Journal and discussion: 5%

## Historical neutrality and evidence
Teachers should not grade students for taking a preferred political position. Grade factual accuracy, source quality, reasoning, constitutional understanding, counterargument, and clarity.

## Sensitive history
Teach slavery, racial violence, the Holocaust, war, terrorism, and recent political conflict factually and age-appropriately. Avoid graphic material that is unnecessary to the learning target. Provide advance notice and alternative access when a student has a documented need.

## AI and research
Students may use approved tools only within teacher rules. Every factual claim, quotation, statistic, and citation must be verified against a trustworthy source. Invented citations receive no evidence credit.

## Credit note
The course is designed as United States History Honors. Integrated government and civic literacy are preparation and reinforcement; they do not constitute a claim that the separate Florida United States Government half-credit has been completed.
