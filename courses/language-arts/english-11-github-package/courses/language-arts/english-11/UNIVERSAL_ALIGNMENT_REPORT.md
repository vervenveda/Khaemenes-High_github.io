# Grade 11 English III — Universal Alignment Verification Report

## Compliance statement

The United States does not have one federal national English curriculum or one national Grade 11 graduation standard. States, districts, private schools, umbrella programs, homeschool evaluators, scholarship programs, and accrediting organizations may impose different requirements. This course is therefore designed for broad portability and transparent documentation rather than making an impossible universal-approval claim.

## Framework coverage

| Framework | Course coverage |
|---|---|
| Common Core ELA/Literacy 11–12 | Close reading, evidence, theme/central idea, structure, perspective, rhetoric, research, argument, explanatory and narrative writing, collaboration, presentation, language, vocabulary, and disciplinary literacy. |
| Florida B.E.S.T. Grade 11 ELA | Literary meaning/style, universal themes, juxtaposition, informational structure, classical speeches/essays, rhetoric, multiple arguments, writing modes, research, oral presentation, media, conventions, vocabulary. |
| SAT Reading and Writing | Information and Ideas; Craft and Structure; Expression of Ideas; Standard English Conventions; textual and quantitative evidence. |
| ACT English and Reading | Production of Writing; Knowledge of Language; Conventions; close reading; main ideas; relationships; craft and structure; integration. |
| AP English Language readiness | Rhetorical situation, claims and evidence, reasoning and organization, style, synthesis, rhetorical analysis, and argument. |
| AP English Literature readiness | Character, setting, structure, narrator/speaker, word choice, imagery, symbols, comparison, poetry, fiction, and drama analysis. |
| IB Language A reference | Literary and nonliterary texts, context, culture, critical literacy, global perspectives, written analysis, comparison, and oral communication. |

## Evidence retained

- 36 weekly plans and quizzes
- 12 unit assessments and 12 teacher-scored major projects
- Diagnostic, midterm, and final examinations
- Literary, rhetorical, comparative, argumentative, research, narrative, oral, and multimedia products
- Reading log, attendance log, gradebook, corrections, revisions, portfolio, and completion certificate

## Local adoption checklist

1. Confirm required instructional hours and credit value.
2. Crosswalk any additional state or program-specific standards.
3. Approve the final reading list and accessibility supports.
4. Preserve teacher-scored writing, research, and presentation evidence.
5. Verify transcript, scholarship, umbrella, district, and accreditation requirements.
6. Do not market the course as AP or IB authorized unless the applicable approval process is completed.

## Design conclusion

The course is advanced and broadly aligned because it requires sustained complex reading, evidence-based interpretation, global and cross-cultural comparison, rhetorical analysis, source-based argument, research synthesis, long-form writing, oral communication, multimedia publication, language control, cumulative assessment, and portfolio defense. It is suitable as a strong English III foundation and can support AP/IB readiness, but it is not automatically equivalent to an authorized AP or IB course.
