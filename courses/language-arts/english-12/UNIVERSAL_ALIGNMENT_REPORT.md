# Grade 12 English IV — Universal Advanced Alignment Verification Report

**Verified:** August 5, 2026  
**Course length:** 36 weeks · 12 units · 180 Monday–Friday lessons

## Compliance statement

The United States does not have one federal national English curriculum or one national Grade 12 graduation standard. States, districts, private schools, umbrella programs, homeschool evaluators, scholarship programs, accrediting organizations, and postsecondary institutions may impose different requirements. This course is designed for broad portability, advanced readiness, and transparent documentation rather than making an impossible universal-approval claim.

## Required evidence retained by the course

- 36 weekly plans, 180 daily lessons, 36 repeatable quizzes, and correction records
- 12 unit assessments and 12 teacher-scored major products
- Beginning diagnostic, 30-question cumulative midterm, and 50-question cumulative final
- Literary, poetic, rhetorical, comparative, argumentative, research, narrative, professional, oral, and multimedia products
- A sustained academic research project and a separate senior capstone contribution
- Long-form novel or drama study, independent seminar, and literary oral defense
- Reading log, attendance or instructional-time log, gradebook, drafts, feedback, revisions, portfolio, and completion certificate
- Graduation portfolio, oral defense, and postsecondary literacy plan

## Design conclusion

This course meets the design target for an advanced, nationally portable English IV curriculum because it integrates independent high-complexity reading; world literature across periods, regions, and media; literary and rhetorical evaluation; source-based argument; quantitative and media literacy; sustained research; college, workplace, and civic communication; oral presentation; accessible digital publication; long-form study; a senior capstone; portfolio defense; and cumulative assessment. It remains subject to local approval and is not automatically equivalent to an authorized AP, IB, Cambridge, dual-enrollment, or accredited course.
